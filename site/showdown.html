How this works, and what the numbers mean

Where the split falls

A GitHub Actions run fetches Yahoo plus nflverse depth, roster and injury data, applies the frozen salary-position-depth regression and the fitted coefficients of variation, and publishes the PSD-repaired latent correlation matrix for each game. That payload is about 7 KB. This page downloads it and does the rest: it draws the scenarios, enumerates every Yahoo-valid roster, screens them for the selected objective, scores the survivors against shared scenarios, and applies the exposure caps.

The feeds never need to be touched again after the model is published.

Portfolio coverage and evaluation

Automatic starts with the highest analytic expectation, then greedily adds lineups that improve the portfolio's best score across scenarios. The first half of the draws selects entries; the other half evaluates the completed set against its single-lineup anchor. These are model-based simulation checks, not historical performance or contest-winning probabilities.

Screening and selection

Expected-points objectives screen by analytic mean. Floor and ceiling objectives screen by approximate P25 and P90, using a lognormal matched to each lineup's mean and variance. The retained candidates are ranked by their simulated percentiles. The experimental upside objective keeps the mean-plus-volatility screen. All modes also reserve high-mean candidates. Screening and greedy portfolio selection are approximations, not a guarantee of the globally best lineup or portfolio. Increase detail to check stability.

Why excluding a player is cheap

Removing a player is a principal submatrix of a positive semi-definite matrix, which is still positive semi-definite. So there is no eigendecomposition and no PSD repair here, and the scenarios are not even redrawn — an exclusion only changes which lineups are legal.

Why your numbers differ from the published run

This page draws its own scenarios from its own generator, so simulated means and percentiles land within Monte Carlo error of the published ones rather than on top of them. The analytic quantities — salary, expected points, the analytic standard deviation, the count of valid rosters — are computed from the same model and match exactly.

Entries marked also published were selected by both runs. On a portfolio this deep that overlap is itself information: an entry that survives two independent scenario sets is one the ranking actually prefers, rather than one that won a near-tie on sampling noise.

Candidate comparison rates

Candidate-best rate is the share of simulations in which an entry ties for the highest score among the screened candidates. Near-best rate is the share within the model's threshold of that best score. These rates depend on the candidate pool and detail setting; they are not estimates of contest-winning probability. Candidate-best rate is especially noisy when there are few matching simulations.

Model

The current correlated lognormal approximation preserves projected means but cannot produce zero or negative scores. Participation and discrete scoring models remain deferred until their inputs and calibration are supported.
